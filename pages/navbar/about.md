    <summary><h2 style="display:inline-block">My Degree</h2></summary>
      <p>As I said above, I have a Bachelor of Science degree in Computer Science with two minors, one in Game Design and the other in Data Science.
      <br>
      I was a direct admit to the Luddy school of Informatics, Computing, and Engineering, so I was able to start studying CS my first semester there. I didn't decide on         my specialization until my second semester. 
      <br> The options were: Artificial Intelligence, Data Science, Foundations, Game Development, Programming Languages, Security, Software Engineering, and Systems.
      <br><br>
        I initially couldn't decide between Game Dev, Software Engineering, and Systems, but I ruled out Game Dev because it seemed too "specialized". I decided to                 pursue Software Engineering, but keep Systems "on the back burner", so to speak: I took classes that overlapped between their requirements whenever I could;                according to my academic advisor, I'd be able to list both on my resume if I completed all the requirements, but it wouldn't officially show up on my transcript            since you can only have one specialization. 
       <br><br>
        At the same time I chose my specialization, though, I decided to pursue a minor in Game Design. Notice the difference: Development vs. Design. While most of                the required classes were the same (one of my friends did specialize in Game Dev, we had a lot of classes together), not all of them were. Development leaned               heavily into the programming aspect of building games while Design covered aspects more broadly: sound and visual art direction/composition, level and mechanic             design, player experience, that sort of stuff. It wasn't totally based on video games either, we also covered board and card games, among others. There was                 even a whole economics class focused specifically on creating and managing economies in games. 
        <br><br>
        When it came time to plan for my last semester of classes, though, I considered the fact that I'd have to take two extra classes to fulfill the requirements for            the Systems specialization that wouldn't even be recognized officially, and I did a little research. I wound up realizing that if I took one particular extra               class, I'd retroactively satisfy all the requirements to complete a Data Science minor, which is exactly what I did.
        <br><br>
        Unfortunately, I wound up "failing" one class my last semester that was required for my degree. Although, technically, I didn't fail, and it wasn't specifically            required -- by that I mean that I ended up getting a "passing" grade as far as credit is concerned, but I didn't need the credit, I needed a certain grade in a             P400 or higher level class that wasn't already being used to fulfill another requirement. 
        <br> 0.7% short overall. Just a few more points on any assignment would have been enough; if the second extra credit assignment (which was nearly identical to the          first, maybe a little harder) hadn't been worth less than half as many points as the first, that would have been enough. But that didn't happen, so I took a brief          summer class online through IU Southeast that fulfilled the same requirement but covered a different subject.  
        <br><br>
        Also, during my senior year of high school, I took Finite mathematics as a Dual Credit course through IU, so I was technically taking the class through IU at the           same time. Which means that my IU transcript claims I attended 10 straight semesters instead of the usual 8; one before I started attending, and one after my               intended graduation. It also means that I had a 4.0 GPA as soon as I started at IU since I got an A in that class.
        <br><br>
        On the note of my graduation, I did not know that I wouldn't actually be graduating on time thanks to that one class until a day or two after the ceremony was              over. The final grades didn't get put in until afterwards, and there was supposed to be a curve which most of the other students did receive, I did not. But on a           positive note, the ceremony, which I did attend, just so happened to take place on May the Fourth -- Star Wars Day! It happens a different day every year, and it           just so happened to be that day the year I graduate. Very cool as a Star Wars Fan, didn't make up for needing to take another class.</p>
